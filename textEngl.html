myFRIEND


Read the text out loud to me:



Mike smoothed the silk lapels of his midnight-blue tuxedo one last time. The piece fit like a glove and had probably cost more than a compact car. Squaring his chin, he put on his best VIP face and stepped into the cavernous, marble-clad lobby of the Grand Eastwoodshire.

He headed straight for the reception desk. Standing there was a lanky clerk in a classic red blazer, looking as though he had been freshly clipped from a department store catalog.

“I am the keynote speaker,” Mike announced in a theatrical baritone. “I’m delivering the eulogy tonight. Where might I find the ballroom?”

“Welcome to the Grand Eastwoodshire, sir. It is an honor to have you.” The receptionist smiled, his voice so sugary-sweet that Mike briefly feared for his molars. “We are hosting several major events this evening. What is the name of your guest of honor?”

Mike’s brain switched to autopilot. “Joe!” he replied, flashing a winning smile.

The clerk typed away enthusiastically. “Joe. Beautiful. And the last name?”

Mike stared into space. The clacking of the keys suddenly sounded like the ticking of a time bomb. Joe. For twenty-five years, he had been his best friend. The man he had gone through thick and thin with for over two decades. But the last name…? Nothing. A total blank. His forehead began to burn. Strands of cold sweat started pouring down under the armpits of his sinfully expensive tuxedo, while the receptionist continued to smile at him with unbearable, angelic patience.

“I… uh… I…” Mike stammered, desperately trying to conjure his best friend’s face in his mind’s eye. Just then, a dark-haired man in his mid-fifties brushed past him.

“Sorry, pal, I’m running incredibly late!” the man called out to the receptionist. “Quick, tell me, where’s Joe McCrea’s party?”

“In the Sussex Hall, sir,” the clerk explained, gesturing elegantly to the right.

McCrea! The name hit Mike like a lightning bolt of pure salvation. “That’s it!” he cried out in sheer relief, the sweat on his brow evaporating instantly. “That’s my party! I’m the speaker for that one!”

The mid-fifties man turned around and shook Mike’s hand so vigorously that his cufflinks rattled. “Brilliant that you made it! Let’s hustle, or the bash will start without us—and that would be an absolute disaster!”

As they stepped into the corridor, a massive wall of sound washed over them. “Man, they’re really tearing the place down,” Mike noted. “The chatter is echoing all the way into the hallway. What a mega-party!”

“When you celebrate, you go all out!” the man in his mid-fifties shouted, his eyes gleaming. “I’m going to dart ahead—after all, I’m playing the second most important role in the room tonight!” With those words, he pushed through the golden entrance doors and vanished into the bustling crowd.

Whoa, Joe has really put on some weight, Mike thought, glancing guiltily at his own protruding belly. Well, none of us are getting any younger. I almost didn't recognize him at first glance.

He fished a slightly crumpled piece of paper from his tuxedo pocket and skimmed his notes: “Your appearance still possesses something outrageously captivating, like a young Adonis…”Well, then. That might be a tiny bit exaggerated for a man with a slight double chin and a shortness of breath. But hey—at a party, you were allowed to glorify the birthday boy a little. Still, he wanted to clear it with Joe real quick. Better safe than sorry.

By now, Mike had fought his way into the heart of the ballroom. In front of the dance floor stood the guest of honor’s table, decorated with delicate little roses. And right there stood the mid-fifties man, stressed out and straightening the last few name cards. Mike approached him from behind. “An absolute sight for sore eyes. You can tell immediately how much heart and soul you poured into organizing this.”

The man spun around, visibly caught up in organizational madness. “Well, the fun didn’t come cheap! But how often do you get to celebrate a silver wedding anniversary? This is an event for the ages. Who knows who’ll even be walking with a cane by the time the golden one rolls around,” he joked.

Mike let out a conspiratorial chuckle. “You can say that again! If the wife knew that our friendship over the last twenty years was just an alibi to pull the wool over her eyes so her husband could secretly play the field… Man, the house would be on fire, wouldn’t it?”

The man froze mid-motion. He stared at Mike in utter disbelief. Then he burst into a roaring laugh and gave him a playful, yet forceful poke in the ribs. “You bet! That would be the absolute end of the world!”

“Don’t worry, my lips are sealed.” Mike raised his hand in a solemn oath. The man grinned broadly: “Give me five!” Their hands clapped together.

“Since we’re so beautifully on the same page, about my speech…” Mike hedged. “Do you think I can really pull off the ‘young Adonis’ bit? I’d hate to rewrite my entire script, otherwise I’ll fumble on the mic and look like an idiot.”

The man patted him jovially on the shoulder. “Not a problem at all, pal! Just deliver it with a wink. Tell everyone: ‘He has the looks of a young Adonis—thankfully, though, he also has enough of a sense of humor not to rub it in our faces all the time!’ That’ll get the crowd laughing instantly.”

Mike beamed. “Magnificent! You truly are unbeatable when it comes to keeping everything perfectly coordinated!”

“Some people just have that extra touch of talent,” the man agreed in high spirits. “I need to go check on the bride!” He scurried off, heading straight toward a lady in a daring excess of ruffles, who was standing next to an older gentleman beneath a giant silver wreath featuring a bold number “25.”

Mike watched, deeply moved, as the man gave the bride an affectionate embrace. A warm sense of relief washed over his chest. How beautiful it was that the two of them got along so well after all these years! Almost like a pair of young lovebirds,he thought, wondering for a split second if his speech contained enough emotional sap. But it was far too late for changes now anyway. Public speaking was not exactly one of his core competencies; messing with the script at this stage would be pure Russian roulette. He absolutely could not afford to embarrass himself—or, more importantly, his best pal—in front of this massive crowd.

Right then, a bright, energetic ting-ting-ting rang out.

The mid-fifties man was enthusiastically tapping his champagne flute with a small spoon, asking the party to take their seats. He was just about to announce Mike’s grand anniversary speech when the bride rudely cut him off: "An empty stomach has no ears! Feast first!"

The next moment, the doors to the Sussex Hall flew open. An army of waiters marched in, laden with massive silver trays piled high with pheasant, turkey, mountains of salad, and steaming gravy boats. Mike managed to snag a seat at a side table. To keep an eye on the head table, he had to crane his neck like a hungry giraffe, but he managed to catch glimpses.

The mid-fifties man was already engrossed in an animated conversation with the bride and his tablemates. The older man on the other side of the bride, however, poked listlessly at his food with the look of a convicted felon. After a while, Mike got the feeling he was witnessing a game of Telephone. Every time two neighbors whispered to each other, they would glance at the bride, chuckle hysterically to themselves, and pass the whisper along to the next person. Meanwhile, the alcohol was flowing in rivers, driving the festive mood in the room through the roof.

By the time the main courses were cleared and dessert arrived, the wave of giggles had reached the other side of the table and was now rolling inexorably back toward the guests of honor.

Finally, the great feast came to an end. The mid-fifties man stood up once more, ready to finally introduce the speaker of the evening. At that exact moment, the wave of gossip reached the grumpy old gentleman next to the bride. He leaned his ear toward his neighbor—but just as Mike had suspected, the old man didn't crack a smile, didn't smirk—nothing.

The guy has all the humor of a prune, Mike muttered to himself. What a dry old bone. Guaranteed to be some unpleasant relative of the bride.

It was time. Time for the big entrance. At the exact second Mike solemnly rose to his feet, a volcano erupted at the head table.

Pure fury broke across the old man’s face. His forehead creased into deep lines of rage, his eyes threatening to pop out of their sockets. And right over Mike’s cheerfully belted out, "Welcome to tonight’s silver anniversary couple!", the old man opened his mouth. A roaring tsunami of anger swept across the banquet tables: "WHO DARES?!"

Except for the bride and Mike, everyone in the room seemed to grasp instantly what this was about. They all stared helplessly at the mid-fifties man. But he didn’t hesitate. Completely unfazed and without blinking an eye, he extended a long index finger and pointed straight across the room right at Mike: "It was him! He’s the one who claimed it!"

Mike had no idea what hit him, but proactively turned beet red anyway.

"That absolute degenerate!" the old man spat, completely out of control. His blood was boiling. With a massive leap, he jumped right onto the white banquet table. Dishes rattled, glasses shattered, and the groom took off at breakneck speed. Like a wild dervish, he balanced between panna cottas and cream cakes, raising his fork like a weapon, heading straight for Mike.

The young blonde next to Mike was the first to sense the danger. She didn’t wait for the furious old man to reach their table. In one fluid motion, she grabbed her bowl and launched her chocolate mousse.

From here on out, time seemed to move in slow motion. The creamy projectile sailed through the air in a high arc and struck the exact center of Mike’s chest with a dull splat. Instantly, his extra-starched tuxedo shirt changed design from polar white to mud brown. That was the starting gun for the entire party. The air was suddenly filled with flying desserts. A stray shot of vanilla pudding clipped Mike’s left shoulder, followed by a perfect payload of blueberry cream that transformed his lapel into a colorful work of art.

By now, the mid-fifties man had rushed around the table, grabbed the completely paralyzed Mike by the arm, and whispered frantically into his ear, "Sorry! But your joke was just so damn good, I had to pass it along! I'm really sorry the groom is losing his mind like this. But it was worth the laugh! The old guy has been dragging himself through this marriage like the walking dead for years—no passion, no opinion, no real sign of life. Your alibi gag fit like a glove! Now leg it, or the old geezer will murder you with his cane!"

With a loud laugh, the mid-fifties man shoved the utterly stunned, sticky Mike out of the ballroom and slammed the heavy wooden door shut behind him. The dull thud of the closing door sealed his fate. Mike was alone.

Slowly, he looked down at himself. He looked like he had lost a close-quarters brawl with an industrial dough mixer. Gradually, the warm, sugary dessert sludge seeped through the thin fabric of his shirt directly onto his skin. His breath came only in short, shallow gasps—the sheer humiliation had squeezed every bit of air out of his lungs. His knees turned to jelly. For weeks, he had polished every phrase, practiced every joke in front of the mirror—and now here he stood, the walking laughingstock of the Grand Eastwoodshire.

In his trembling hand, he was still holding his cue card. A damp smudge of blueberry juice was currently eating its way through the word "Adonis."

Searching for a shred of dignity, his eyes wandered down the endless, sterile hallway. But instead of salvation, he found the naked, brutal truth. His gaze locked onto a large, elegant display stand right next to the adjacent door. "Today: Silver Wedding Anniversary of Joe McRae in the Sussex Hall."

Mike’s breath stopped entirely. Every last bit of color drained from his cheeks until he was white as a sheet. The blood in his veins seemed to freeze. McCrea and McRae. One tiny letter, one gigantic disaster.

His hands flew to his face in sheer disbelief. His knees finally gave out for good. Like a felled tree, he collapsed in on himself, sliding down the wall and hitting his head with a dull thud against the plush carpet. There he lay, defeated by his own utter cluelessness. For years, he had played the perfect alibi for a man whose face he wouldn't even have recognized anymore, only to burst into the wrong party at the final stretch.

At this exact moment of absolute defeat, footsteps approached. An older couple walked elegantly past him, looking askance at the pathetic heap sitting on the floor, covered in icing.

"Look at that, Joe," the woman whispered to her husband, raising her eyebrows in disapproval. "Now they’ve got people offering desperate prayers to Allah right in the hallways of the Grand Eastwoodshire."

Her husband proudly squeezed her arm, not even deigning to look, and smiled. "Well, darling, thankfully we have no need for that. After all, you and I have spent a quarter of a century standing side by side in unwavering devotion!"
